Your Top 3 Priorities at Level 6
These are the three moves that create the most leverage at your level.
1
Restructure your calendar so that strategy-first is structural, not aspirational.
Remove yourself from every recurring operational meeting. Not gradually — structurally. Block three days per week for strategic work: vision, positioning, growth decisions, key relationships, and long-horizon thinking. If your calendar does not enforce the separation, your intentions will not either. The business will pull you back in every time unless the calendar makes it physically impossible.
2
Build a quarterly strategic agenda.
Not a to-do list. A direction. What are the three strategic questions you are answering this quarter? Where is the business going in the next 12 months — and what decisions only you can make to get it there? The operational metrics belong to the team, reviewed weekly. The strategic metrics belong to you, reviewed quarterly. Separate them clearly or they bleed into each other and neither gets the attention it requires.
3
Define your technology roadmap.
At Level 6, Program is a strategic investment category, not an operational convenience. Which functions should be automated in the next 12 months? Where is the organization spending expensive human hours on machine-appropriate work? What is the technology direction — not the tool selection, but the organizational posture toward AI and automation? You decide direction. Your team decides deployment.
 
The Identity Shift
The shift required to leave Level 6 is this:
“I am responsible for what happens day to day”
↓
“I am responsible for where this business is going.”
Operations is the team’s job. Vision, positioning, growth, and culture are yours. Until that separation is made structurally — in the calendar, in the role definition, in the daily rhythm — it does not exist. Aspirational separation is not separation. Structural separation is.
The owner who stays close to operations is not more informed. They are more distracted. The work of a Level 6 owner is not to know everything that is happening. It is to build the direction that everything else is moving toward. Closeness to operations costs you the altitude you need to lead strategically.
 
The Cost of Staying
Every year you stay at Level 6:
— Your strategic thinking is crowded out by operational noise
— The business hits its ceiling because your bandwidth IS the ceiling
— You are building an operator’s resume, not an owner’s legacy
— The business cannot grow past the size you can personally oversee
— You delay the compounding returns that only come from strategic-level work
Level 6 is deceptive. The business is successful. You are respected. Your team is strong. From the outside, nothing is wrong. From the inside, you know you are spending your best hours on things that no longer require your best thinking. The cost is not what you are doing wrong — it is what you are not doing at all.
