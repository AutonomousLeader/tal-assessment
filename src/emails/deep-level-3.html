Your Top 3 Priorities at Level 3
These are the three moves that create the most leverage at your level.
1
Build and communicate the Decision Authority Matrix.
List every type of decision your team currently escalates to you. Sort each one into three levels: Level 1 (they decide and act, no notification required), Level 2 (they decide and act, then inform you afterward), Level 3 (they bring the decision to you before acting). Most of what currently hits your desk is Level 1 or Level 2. Nobody ever told your team that — so they default to escalating everything.
2
Enforce the matrix by stepping back.
The hard part is not writing the matrix. The hard part is honoring it. When a team member brings you a Level 1 decision — and they will, out of habit — ask “what would you do?” and approve their answer rather than providing your own. Every time you answer a question your team could handle, you train them to keep asking. Every time you redirect, you train them to decide.
3
Write your first five operating principles.
Your team escalates because they do not have a framework for deciding without you. Write five operating principles — the situations that come up most often where your team currently asks for direction. Format: “When X happens, we do Y.” These give your team something to reach for when you are not in the room. They stop guessing and start deciding.
 
The Identity Shift
The shift required to leave Level 3 is this:
“I approve the decisions”
↓
“I build decision-makers.”
The short-term speed of making the decision yourself is real. In the long run it is catastrophically expensive. Every decision you make personally is a decision your team did not develop the capacity to make. The short-term speed compounds into long-term dependence.
Your team has stopped developing judgment because yours is always available. The fix is not finding better people. The fix is giving the people you have the authority, the principles, and the room to develop their own judgment — and then getting out of the way.
 
The Cost of Staying
Every year you stay at Level 3:
— Your team grows in size but not in capability — they never develop judgment
— You cannot grow past your own decision-making bandwidth
— Every vacation, every sick day, every conference is a liability
— You are producing followers, not leaders
— The business gets bigger but you do not get freer
Level 3 is where many business owners stay permanently. The business looks successful from the outside. From the inside, the owner is more trapped than they were at Level 1 — because now they have a team that depends on them for every real decision.
